High Society New Buffalo, then the whole client portfolio. 2026-09-03. Everything below was measured this session. Where something is unproven it says so.

[agency-staging-host] appears in three different clients' live markup, and it is the host that fails TLS and returns 404. [agency-domain] is your own domain. This is a build-pipeline leak, not nine unrelated client mistakes. One fix at the platform level clears all nine.

Only 5 of 29 clients are completely clean.

Confirmed defects across the portfolio

4 clients have a broken canonical on the exact page their Google listing designates:

A location page canonicalising to the homepage tells Google to rank the homepage instead of the store page. Root'd's is the worst of the four: noindexed and serving an unconfigured menu embed.

12 clients have a designated landing page with zero referring domains while their own homepage holds between 62 and 396. Five different brands: High Society (New Buffalo, Big Rapids, Birch Run, Saline), Flora Terra ×3, MaryJane's ×3, Lava Leaf, Mountain Source.

MaryJane's, all three locations: an infinite redirect loop for any cookieless client. /stores/ON/<store> → /setshop/<id>?redirect=… → back, forever. With a cookie jar it resolves cleanly in two hops. So it is a stateless-crawler trap. Whether Googlebot specifically trips it is not directly verified.

High Society trailing-slash bug: all six /shop/<city>/ listing URLs carry a trailing slash that 301s. Mount Pleasant's 186 referring domains sit entirely on the no-slash form the listing does not designate.

10 clients have a thin Google Business Profile, six of them declaring a single service option against a benchmark median of 4 across 341 measured profiles.

High Society New Buffalo specifically

It ranks 8th of 40 for "dispensary near me", not 21st — Local Falcon ranks on Share of Local Voice; I spent much of this session reading ARP, which averages only the points where a business is found.

Three confirmed defects, in severity order:

  1. The landing page canonicalises to a dead staging URL and its title says "Big Rapids". One of eight High Society location pages; the other seven are correct.
  2. Zero presence on the mainstream directory tier. YellowPages, Superpages, DexKnows, Hotfrog, CitySquares — Lume is on all five, High Society on none. Verified as real absence: YellowPages renders a 37-row New Buffalo index (the control query rendered 68) naming competitors and not this one.
  3. Thin profile: 1 service option, 6 attributes, against top-half medians of 4 and 11.

The sharpest single number: at the grid point 157 feet from its own front door it ranks 4th (29 Aug 2026 scan; between 2nd and 11th across the 37 scans of its own grid).

The age gate — a confirmed defect with UNPROVEN ranking impact

High Society's site renders 352 characters after full JavaScript execution. No H1 on any of the 20 crawlable pages. A link-following crawler discovers 20 URLs; the sitemaps declare 7,395. Competitors render 10,000–14,000 characters with a real H1 and full contact details. 17 of 29 clients render under 1,500 characters.

I then tested whether this actually costs ranking, and it does not clearly do so. Comparing blocked against healthy clients within the same keyword, by percentile rank in field:

KeywordBlockedHealthy
cannabis near me13.3%7.0%blocked worse
dispensary near me6.6%6.5%blocked marginally worse
weed near me17.3%30.2%blocked better

Spearman of rendered characters against percentile rank: 0.014. Nothing.

My first version of this test appeared to show blocked clients ranking better; it pooled 29 easy "weed near me" scans in fields of 1–38 with one each of the harder keywords. Invalid, caught before reporting. The corrected result is the one above.

So: fix the age gate because a site with no H1 and 20 discoverable pages is broken. Do not sell it as the cause of the ranking gap — the data does not support that.

Ruled out, with the evidence

  • Citations. n=353 across 29 markets. Raw ρ +0.173, but −0.046 under control, and 0.018 among single-location businesses. The pooled effect was chain brand-links credited to every branch. Link-scheme junk tracks rank better than real directories, which is backwards for a causal story. Citations are a marketing-spend proxy.
  • My own landing-page-authority theory. ρ 0.929 at n=6, −0.162 at n=81. Refuted.
  • Reviews. Velocity beats total but explains 18% of variance. Lume ranks 2nd on a third of High Society's review count at a nearly identical rate.
  • Proximity. All pins within 0.51 miles on the same road.
  • The July cliff. Pure displacement: nothing on High Society's profile changed, and it gained reviews while dropping.

The only variables surviving full statistical control are GBP service-options count (+0.188, p=.0014) and description length (+0.131, p=.027).

What to do

Platform level, fixes nine clients at once:

  1. Purge staging hostnames from production builds. Add a release check that fails the build if staging. or [agency-staging-host] appears in any emitted <head>.

Per client:

  1. Fix the four broken canonicals; self-canonicalise location pages.
  2. Remove noindex,nofollow from Root'd In The 510 and configure its menu embed.
  3. Point Google listings at the no-slash URL variant where the authority actually sits.
  4. Fix the MaryJane's cookieless redirect loop.
  5. Set the missing GBP service options on the ten thin profiles.

High Society New Buffalo:

  1. All of the above, plus claim the six general directories.
