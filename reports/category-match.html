Does Google primary category associate with where a business ranks?

A within-market test on 6,440 business-observations from 121 cached Local Falcon grid scans across 29 markets. Entirely offline — no API calls, no spend.

6,440 rows 3,425 businesses 121 reports 29 markets 106 rows with no category generated 2026-09-02

01Verdict

Yes — primary category associates with rank, and it is by a wide margin the strongest signal found in this dataset so far. Holding market, query and day constant (each grid scan is its own stratum), a business whose Google primary category is cannabis_store or cannabis_club sits 16.7 percentile points above a non-matching business in the same field (95% CI 14.0 to 19.4; stratified Mann-Whitney A = 0.59, z = 4.51, p = 6.5×10−6). The gap favours the exact-match category in 88 of 97 reports (sign test p = 3.7×10−18).

It survives control. With report fixed effects plus reviews, rating and distance-to-grid-centre, the exact-match coefficient is 14.29 points (SE 2.13, p = 1.7×10−9) — barely moved from the uncontrolled 15.99. Restricting to businesses whose pin actually sits inside the scanned grid radius — the fairest comparison, since it strips out distant name-matching listings — the coefficient grows to 33.50 points (p = 2.8×10−15) and exact-category businesses beat non-matching ones in 88% of head-to-head pairs. Reviews contribute about 1.07 points per unit of log-reviews and rating is not distinguishable from zero, so category is roughly an order of magnitude larger than either.

Three qualifications, stated plainly. (1) The effect is monotone but not binary — the adjacent bucket (smoke shop, vaporizer store, herb shop, alternative medicine) lands cleanly between the two, 8.27 points above non-match and 7.36 points below exact. (2) It is absent for “weed near me” (6.29 points, p = 0.3339), where fields are tiny and 81.5% of everything Google returns has the literal word in its name — on that query, name-matching visibly overrides category. (3) Position-within-field is conditional on being in the field at all; this design cannot speak to entry, and every statement here is an association, not a demonstrated cause.

02What was tested, and how

One row per (report × business). The outcome is within-field percentile rank by Share of Local Voice: 0 = worst in that grid’s field, 100 = best, mid-ranks for ties. Every test runs inside a single keyword; the headline test runs inside a single report, so market, query and scan date are held identical by construction.

Method decisions, and why
  • Primary category = the FIRST key of the report's categories object. Local Falcon returns the categories in Google's own order; internal check: cannabis_store is first in 4096 of the 4151 rows that carry it, and where it is not first the first key is the business's obvious real primary (Zaza Smoke Shop → smoke_shop, Exotic Vapes → tobacco_shop). Not independently verified against Google's own "primary category" field.
  • Percentile rank = mid-rank percentile of SoLV inside the report's own field, 0 = worst in field, 100 = best. A second outcome, percentile of ATRP (average total rank position, which uses the full rank not just the top-3 window SoLV measures), is reported alongside because 60% of SoLV values are exactly 0.
  • Every test is run WITHIN a keyword. Nothing is pooled across keywords except the stratified within-report statistic, which holds keyword constant by construction (each stratum is one report = one keyword).
  • found_in is NOT used as a control: it is a component of SoLV, so conditioning on it conditions on a mediator.
  • Field membership is itself a selection: the places object only holds businesses Google returned somewhere in the grid. Position-within-field results are conditional on entry.

Bucket definitions, derived from the observed frequency table

exact — cannabis_store, cannabis_club. The two categories Google itself assigns a cannabis retail storefront; no other category key in the corpus contains cannabis, marijuana, weed, dispensary or hemp.

adjacent — smoke_shop, tobacco_shop, tobacco_supplier, vaporizer_store, hookah_store, hookah_bar, herb_shop, herbal_medicine_store, herbalist, organic_drug_store, alternative_medicine_practitioner, alternative_medicine_clinic, holistic_medicine_practitioner, naturopathic_practitioner, homeopath, homeopathic_pharmacy, pharmacy, drugstore, grow_shop, hydroponics_equipment_supplier.

non — every other primary category (201+ distinct keys, from store and gift_shop down to lake, bus_stop and police_department).

A narrower adjacency variant that drops the medicine-dispensing categories (pharmacy, drugstore, homeopath, naturopath, alternative medicine) is reported as a sensitivity check; it changes nothing material.

Does a secondary cannabis category rescue a non-matching primary?

Only 50 rows in the whole corpus have a non-cannabis primary but a cannabis category further down the list, against 1,061 without. Directionally positive, roughly half the size of the primary-category effect, and thin enough that it should be treated as a hint rather than a finding.

09The Black Bear case study

The hypothesis that prompted this test was that Black Bear’s zeros are a category effect — hemp retailers wearing the wrong label. That is not what the data says. All three Pennsylvania locations carry cannabis_store as their Google primary category, exactly like the dispensaries beating them.

What the 12 zero-SoLV Black Bear rows actually are
  • 3 rows — the three “weed near me” scans on their own grids. Genuinely absent.
  • 9 rows — the same locations observed from a sibling’s grid 33–49 miles away (Grove City seen from the Clarion scan, and so on), plus one unrelated Winona listing seen from New Buffalo 292 miles off. A business scoring 0 in a grid centred on a different city 40 miles away is normal geography, not a ranking failure.

So the premise “SoLV 0.00 in every market we have for them” does not hold. The real finding is narrower and more interesting: a query-term effect, not a category effect. In these three rural Pennsylvania markets there is essentially no local business named or optimised around “weed”, so Google fills the pack with distant listings that carry the literal word — a wholesaler, a police department beat called “Youngstown Northside Weed”, several “Weed Man” lawn-care franchises, and delivery brands hundreds of miles away. 12Limits and what is not established

  • Association, not causation. Nothing here is an intervention. Category is chosen by operators and moderated by Google; better-run businesses may both pick the right category and rank well for reasons this data cannot separate.
  • Selection on field entry. The places object holds only businesses Google returned somewhere in the grid. Every result is conditional on entry, and a non-match business that got in may be unusual in ways that attenuate the estimate rather than inflate it.
  • “Primary” is inferred from key order in the API response, not read from a labelled field. The internal check is strong (cannabis_store is first in 4,096 of the 4,151 rows carrying it, and where it is not first the first key is the business’s obvious real primary) but it is not independent confirmation.
  • Repeated scans. Some markets are scanned many times, especially New Buffalo. Report fixed effects and report-clustered SEs absorb this for the regression, and the stratified test treats each report separately, but the raw per-keyword row counts still double-count those markets.
  • SoLV is 60% zeros (3,841 of 6,440 rows). Percentile ranking handles the ties with mid-ranks, and an ATRP-based percentile is carried in the data as a cross-check, but a floor-heavy outcome limits power in small fields.
  • The adjacency list is a judgment call. A narrower variant is reported and changes nothing material, but the boundary between “adjacent” and “non” is not a fact in the data.
  • Not tested here: whether changing a category changes rank; whether category matters for field entry (a different design); anything about the 17 one-off New Buffalo keyword variants, which are excluded from every test and reported only in the corpus totals.
