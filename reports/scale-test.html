Do citations predict local rank?

29 client markets · 86 geo-grids · 353 businesses measured · generated 2026-09-03

No — citations are not one of the most important local-ranking factors, but they are not nothing either. Across 29 client markets, 86 market×keyword grids and 2,236 distinct businesses, 353 businesses were sampled from the top and bottom of every grid and measured on citations, links and Google Business Profile completeness. Against SoLV, total referring domains scores ρ = 0.173 (n = 299, significant) and citations on recognised directories — the thing the word actually means — ρ = 0.188 (n = 299, significant). Those are statistically real at this sample size but weak in size, and they rank 10th and 7th of 15 measured variables. The three strongest are Distance from grid centre (grid radii) (ρ = -0.511), GBP service-options count (ρ = 0.293), GBP points at the homepage (vs a deeper page) (ρ = -0.254). Citations are essentially uncorrelated with distance (ρ = -0.039), so the geometry control barely moves them — holding distance constant they read ρ = 0.185 (significant). The control that does move them is review volume: holding distance, review count and rating constant, citations fall to ρ = 0.101 (n = 290, not significant), while review count itself holds at ρ = 0.321 after the geometry control. Two further cuts finish it off. Restricted to single-location businesses — where the domain's links actually belong to that one storefront rather than to a chain's brand site — raw citation count falls to ρ = 0.018 (n = 238, p = 0.786), i.e. nothing at all. And the pattern across link types is backwards for a causal story: link-scheme domains score ρ = 0.180 after every control while non-scheme domains score ρ = -0.123. If citations earned rank, real directories would help and machine-generated junk would not; instead the junk tracks rank slightly better, which is what you see when the variable is really measuring how much this operator spends on marketing rather than a ranking mechanism. On this evidence the owner’s belief that citations are one of the most important local-ranking factors is not supported — distance from grid centre (grid radii) is 3.0× and review count is 1.5× the size of the citation effect. Directory citations specifically survive three controls (ρ = 0.139, p = 0.019) but not the fourth: add chain size — how many storefronts share the domain — and they fall to ρ = 0.089 (p = 0.132), while total citations go to ρ = -0.046 (p = 0.438). Under that fullest control the only things left standing are GBP service-options count (ρ = 0.188), GBP description length (ρ = 0.131) and Link-scheme referring domains (ρ = 0.124) — and the link-scheme count on that list is a symptom of spend, not a lever to pull. So: keep the listings accurate because wrong data actively hurts, but fund the Google Business Profile and the review engine first. Equally, the n = 6 New Buffalo reading of ρ = −0.571 was noise: at this scale the sign is positive, just small.

Partial correlation — the control that matters

Why this control exists. Every grid is centred on the client's own storefront. A competitor two miles away is scored on the client's grid, so its SoLV is partly a statement about where it sits, not about how well it ranks. Distance from the grid centre is therefore a property of the instrument, and any variable that looks predictive before it is held constant has to be re-checked after.

The landing-page-authority theory does not replicate. A prior n = 6 reading suggested rank tracks the authority of the exact page the Google profile points at (ρ = 0.929). Measured here on the 81 businesses whose profile designates a city-named page, referring domains to that exact page score ρ = -0.162 (p = 0.147) — the wrong sign, and nothing after controls (ρ = 0.139). What does show up raw is the choice of page: profiles pointing at the homepage rather than a deeper page score ρ = -0.254 (p < .001), i.e. deep-page designation looks better. But that is confounded with chain size — brands build location pages, single-site operators link their homepage — and once chain size is held constant it fades to ρ = -0.105 (p = 0.077). Treat it as unresolved, not proven.

Method, cost and what is not proven

  • Field. 86 Local Falcon reports (of 86 scan rows; one row in the source CSV carries (not issued) instead of a report URL and has no key to fetch) were read back, giving 4,166 market×keyword×business observations over 2,236 distinct businesses. Reading an existing report costs zero Local Falcon credits: usable credits were [redacted] before and [redacted] after (delta 0). No scan was ever run.
  • Sample. Top 3 and bottom 3 by SoLV in each of the 86 grids, deduped by place_id, giving 353 businesses. Ties break on found_in then place_id, so the selection is deterministic.
  • Citations. backlinks/referring_domains/live against each business's own website domain, one call per distinct domain (260 domains carry 353 businesses). total_count is exact at any limit, so the headline citation count is complete; the real-vs-link-scheme split is read off the top 100 referring domains by backlink volume.
  • Not the business's own site. Where the Google profile's website field points at a URL shortener or a marketplace (Weedmaps, Dutchie, Leafly, a Facebook page), citations are recorded as unmeasured, never as zero — measuring weedmaps.com's link graph would score the marketplace, not the store. That is why n is below 353 on every link variable.
  • Location pages. backlinks/backlinks/live filtered url_to like %city-slug%, run only for the 81 businesses whose profile URL path actually names their city. 81 completed. A business without a city-named location page is unmeasured on this variable, not a zero.
  • Cost. DataForSEO total $[redacted] against a $[redacted] cap enforced in code, which reserves each call's maximum price before issuing it and aborts rather than exceed. The cap never bound.

What this does not establish. This is observational correlation across 29 markets in one vertical, not a causal experiment: nothing here proves that adding citations would not help a specific under-cited store, only that across this field citation count does not order businesses by rank once geography is held constant. Referring domains are a proxy for citations — a directory listing that never links is invisible to it. The real-vs-scheme split is a classification by domain pattern, not a human review of each domain. And SoLV is measured on grids centred on the client, so it describes visibility around the client's own trade area rather than a neutral view of each competitor's home turf. A handful of businesses sit more than 100 grid radii from the centre — Google returning a distant same-brand location at a grid edge; rank-based statistics absorb them, but they are visible as the right-hand tail of the distance chart.
