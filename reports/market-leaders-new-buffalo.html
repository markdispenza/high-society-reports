The answer

Exactly one measure out of 36 separates leaders from our clients in the leaders' favour and survives multiplicity correction: the leader's website serves real text to a crawler and ours does not. (A second measure clears correction pointing the other way — our clients' GBP descriptions are longer — and that one is our own template ceiling, not a leader behaviour; see Design artifacts.) Median rendered page text is 4,069 characters for the market leader against 352 for our client — the leader higher in – of 1 markets. On the server HTML the same gap is Wilcoxon p=–, Holm-adjusted p=–, and it survives collapsing the 1 markets to 1 independent client brands (p=–).

But it is not shown to be what wins. The 0 markets where our client already holds #1 are the control, and there our client's site is just as thin (median – characters) and wins anyway. Comparing only non-client businesses — the 1 that beat our client against the 0 that lost to it — no measure at all separates winners from losers once multiplicity is corrected (best raw p=– on GBP service options, Holm-adjusted p=–). So thin rendering is a consistent property of our client roster, not a demonstrated cause of losing.

Measure by measure, with the paired statistics

Leader and client sit in the same grid, the same week, on the same keyword, so the design is paired and everything that varies between markets is differenced out inside the pair. Continuous measures: Wilcoxon signed-rank (direction and magnitude, no normality assumption; exact enumeration at n≤20 without ties) with the sign test alongside because Wilcoxon assumes a symmetric difference distribution and the sign test does not. Binary measures: McNemar exact on the discordant pairs. Holm-Bonferroni within each family, because 37 measures at α=.05 buys a false positive by construction. No t-test (n≈20, counts are skewed) and no multivariate regression (n will not support one — over-fitting is exactly what produced the discarded n=6 theory).

Is it a winner property, or a client signature?

A paired test can only say the other side differs from our client. It cannot tell “winners do X” apart from “our clients don't do X, and neither does anybody else”. The 0 client-led markets settle it: there the non-client side is a loser. Comparing the 1 businesses that beat our client against the 0 that lost to it isolates winning from being-our-client.

What does NOT separate them

A null is worth as much as a hit here, and several of these contradict things this investigation previously believed.

  • Google Business Profile completeness — all of it. Service options: leader median – vs client –, p=–. Total attributes p=–. Claimed status, hours completeness, book-online link and Q&A count: identical or near-identical on both sides. The earlier finding that service-options count was the strongest surviving predictor does not reproduce on this paired design.
  • Backlink authority, both axes. Landing-page referring domains p=–; site-root referring domains p=– — our clients' roots are, if anything, slightly stronger. The “starved landing page” rate is identical at 0.00 on both sides.
  • Reviews and rating. Reviews p=– (our clients have more, median – vs –). Rating p=–.
  • Chain vs single location. 0.00 of leaders and 0.00 of clients are multi-location, p=–.
  • The size of the gap does not track the size of the ranking gap. Spearman of each measure's leader-minus-client delta against the SoLV delta: rendered text 0.000, landing-page referring domains 0.000, service options 0.000. A bigger deficit does not go with a bigger loss.
