The answer

Exactly one measure out of 36 separates leaders from our clients in the leaders' favour and survives multiplicity correction: the leader's website serves real text to a crawler and ours does not. (A second measure clears correction pointing the other way — our clients' GBP descriptions are longer — and that one is our own template ceiling, not a leader behaviour; see Design artifacts.) Median rendered page text is 5,831 characters for the market leader against 352 for our client — the leader higher in 14 of 17 markets. On the server HTML the same gap is Wilcoxon p=0.002, Holm-adjusted p=0.029, and it survives collapsing the 19 markets to 13 independent client brands (p=0.001).

But it is not shown to be what wins. The 10 markets where our client already holds #1 are the control, and there our client's site is just as thin (median 352 characters) and wins anyway. Comparing only non-client businesses — the 19 that beat our client against the 10 that lost to it — no measure at all separates winners from losers once multiplicity is corrected (best raw p=0.015 on No canonical tag at all, Holm-adjusted p=0.539). So thin rendering is a consistent property of our client roster, not a demonstrated cause of losing.

Ranked by how consistently each measure separates them

  1. Crawlable page text. Leaders 5,831 chars vs our clients 352. 10 of our clients render under 1500 characters against 2 leaders. The only measure to clear Holm correction in the paired test.
  2. Staging hostnames in the production <head>. 7 of our clients carry one; zero leaders do (p=0.016, Holm p=0.281). A one-sided defect with no leader instance — it does not survive multiplicity, but nothing on the leader side offsets it.
  3. Nothing else. Every remaining measure is a null or points the other way. Full table below.

Measure by measure, with the paired statistics

Leader and client sit in the same grid, the same week, on the same keyword, so the design is paired and everything that varies between markets is differenced out inside the pair. Continuous measures: Wilcoxon signed-rank (direction and magnitude, no normality assumption; exact enumeration at n≤20 without ties) with the sign test alongside because Wilcoxon assumes a symmetric difference distribution and the sign test does not. Binary measures: McNemar exact on the discordant pairs. Holm-Bonferroni within each family, because 37 measures at α=.05 buys a false positive by construction. No t-test (n≈20, counts are skewed) and no multivariate regression (n will not support one — over-fitting is exactly what produced the discarded n=6 theory).

Is it a winner property, or a client signature?

A paired test can only say the other side differs from our client. It cannot tell “winners do X” apart from “our clients don't do X, and neither does anybody else”. The 10 client-led markets settle it: there the non-client side is a loser. Comparing the 19 businesses that beat our client against the 10 that lost to it isolates winning from being-our-client.

What does NOT separate them

A null is worth as much as a hit here, and several of these contradict things this investigation previously believed.

  • Google Business Profile completeness — all of it. Service options: leader median 3 vs client 4, p=0.729. Total attributes p=0.322. Claimed status, hours completeness, book-online link and Q&A count: identical or near-identical on both sides. The earlier finding that service-options count was the strongest surviving predictor does not reproduce on this paired design.
  • Backlink authority, both axes. Landing-page referring domains p=0.868; site-root referring domains p=0.560 — our clients' roots are, if anything, slightly stronger. The “starved landing page” rate is identical at 0.53 on both sides.
  • Reviews and rating. Reviews p=0.490 (our clients have more, median 619 vs 531). Rating p=0.102.
  • Chain vs single location. 0.68 of leaders and 0.63 of clients are multi-location, p=1.000.
  • The size of the gap does not track the size of the ranking gap. Spearman of each measure's leader-minus-client delta against the SoLV delta: rendered text -0.109, landing-page referring domains -0.150, service options 0.142. A bigger deficit does not go with a bigger loss.

Design artifacts — reported, never ranked as findings

every grid in this set is centred on the client's own pin

client distance from grid centre across 29 markets: 0, 0.0001, 0.0088, 0.0298 miles; leader distances run 0.03-15.56 miles

the "leaders are further from the grid centre, 19 of 19, p<.001" row is a property of how the scans were commissioned, not evidence about the businesses, and is excluded from the findings table. It does carry one real implication: the client sits at the centre of its own measurement grid and still loses Share of Local Voice, so the loss is not explained by the client being peripheral.

client GBP descriptions cluster against a template ceiling

23 of 29 client descriptions are >=690 characters (the field caps near 750); only 8 of 29 leader descriptions are

the significant "client descriptions are longer" result measures our own fill-the-field habit, not a leader behaviour. Its honest reading is a NULL in the other direction: description length is already maxed on the client side and the client still loses, which refutes description length as an available lever.

the 19 contested markets carry only 13 distinct client brands

4twentymarket.com x2, floraterraca.com x2, highsocietydispo.com x3, maryjanes.ca x3

sibling locations share one website, one CMS template and one GBP-filling habit, so the per-market test counts one agency decision several times. Every headline is reported twice: per market (n=19) and per client brand (n=13).
