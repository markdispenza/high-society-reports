How local ranking actually works

Built from 121 grids, 29 markets, 213,481 business-point observations

Built from 121 Local Falcon grids, 29 markets, 3,423 businesses, 213,481 business-point observations, plus 341 Google profiles and full backlink enumeration. Every claim below was measured this session. Where something is unproven or contested it says so.

The three-line answer

  1. Distance is 47% of it. Measured against each business's proximity ordering at each grid point, not raw miles. The curve is spent by two miles.
  2. Category is a gate. Wrong primary category caps you. Right category leaves everything else still to be earned.
  3. Review mass explains most of what's left — and when you hold reviews constant, twelve of the twenty-three non-review factors that correlate raw collapse to nothing; eleven survive.

Everything else the industry sells is null in this data.

1. Distance — 47% of rank, and it's over by two miles

Pooled Spearman 0.686 against proximity ordering; 0.574 against raw miles. The gap between those two numbers is competitive density — two miles is strong in a sparse market and weak in a dense one. Positive in 115 of 120 grids.

Implication: you cannot out-optimise geography beyond about two miles. Coverage past that comes from more locations, not better SEO.

2. Category — the gate

Holding market, query and day identical, a business whose Google primary category is cannabis_store sits +16.68 percentile points above a non-matching business in the same field.

  • Survives report fixed effects plus reviews, rating and distance: +14.29 points, p = 1.7e-9
  • Restricted to businesses inside the scanned radius: +33.50 points, winning 88% of pairs
  • Monotone: exact > adjacent > non-matching, every step significant
  • Roughly an order of magnitude larger than reviews (+1.07 per log-review) or rating (~zero)
  • Favours the exact category in 88 of 97 grids

But it is a gate, not a lever. Bloomery Cannabis in New Buffalo has the correct category and 14,817 reviews and sits at the 14th percentile of its field with a SoLV of 0 ("dispensary near me", field of 29; category-match/out/category-analysis.json → counter_examples.exact_match_at_the_bottom). Getting the category right removes a cap; it does not win anything.

Exception — "weed near me". Null (+2.2, p = 0.53), because 81.5% of every listing Google returns for that query has "weed" in its name. On that query the field is assembled by string match, so category has nothing left to separate. That is also why a lawn-care franchise outranks dispensaries on it.

3. The residual — who beats their geography

Strip out distance and rank every business by how much better it does than its location predicts.

Over-performers: median 572 reviews, 4.9 stars. None with zero reviews. Royal Weed beats its geography from 47.7 miles out. Under-performers: median 48 reviews, 4.75 stars. Four with zero reviews.

Null throughout, at every stage: landing-page referring domains, root referring domains, rendered page text, server HTML text, H1, NAP on page, JSON-LD, redirect hops, hours, Q&A, amenities, payment attributes, category count, real-directory citations.

Survivors the table above does not show, recorded so they are not lost (proximity-model/out/proximity-model.json → factors, partial on reviews, q < .05): book-online link +0.185 (present = worse), and in the page-health family measured on 58 businesses only, thin render +0.331 and canonical defect +0.397 (defect = worse). That family is the 29 clients and their 29 market leaders, and the leader-vs-non-client test found no page measure that separates winners among non-clients, so these read as the client signature rather than a rank lever. Link-scheme domain count also survives at −0.164 in the direction that says junk links track a better residual, which is the wrong direction for a causal story.

The causal caveat, and it is not small. Reviews and rank are a loop: ranking well brings footfall, which brings reviews. Nothing in observational grid data breaks that loop. Review mass is the best predictor in the dataset; it is not proven to be a cause.

4. What this rules out

Every one of these was tested properly, at scale, with controls, and came back null:

citations (ρ 0.018 among single-location businesses) · backlinks on both axes · landing-page authority (my own theory, ρ 0.929 at n=6 → −0.162 at n=81) · business-name keywords (+0.77 percentile, p=0.34, n=702) · rendered page text · H1 and on-page NAP · photos · attributes · hours · Q&A · category count · book-online links · grid coverage.

5. The scoreboard, in order of what to actually do

  1. Primary category must be cannabis_store. A gate. Check every location.
  2. Reviews are the engine. Best predictor in the dataset by a distance, direction unproven.
  3. Rating, claimed status, service options. Small, real, survive review control. Cheap.
  4. Don't point the listing at your homepage. Associates with a worse residual.
  5. Fix outright defects — dead canonicals, staging hosts, redirect loops. Not statistically proven to cost rank, but they are broken and free to fix.
  6. Accept geography. Beyond two miles, coverage is a real-estate decision.
