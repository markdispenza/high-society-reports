Rules to avoid

Every method that wasted time or money, with what it cost to learn

Each rule states what was tried, what it cost, and what to do instead. Extend as the sweep runs.

  1. Do not use DataForSEO Content Analysis for NAP/citation work. Its "citation" index is a brand-mention crawl dominated by spun spam: brand + city returned 79,425 matches, 98 of the top 100 were casino-slot pages; the phone number returned 0. $[redacted] for nothing. Use SERP + direct probes.
  2. Do not run quoted phone numbers on Bing organic. Bing ignores the quotes and returns area-code trivia. $[redacted]. Bing Places itself needs a direct map probe.
  3. Budget site: queries at 5x. Any Google operator multiplies the SERP price by 5 ($[redacted] per 10 results instead of $[redacted]). Fine at scale, but do not put site: in the wide-depth family.
  4. Google returns ~30 results for a quoted phone regardless of depth. Depth 100 costs nothing extra when fewer come back (billed per SERP returned), but plan recall around 30, not 100.
  5. Google loosens site: when combined with quoted phrases. The Facebook probe returned TikTok and LinkedIn rows. Always post-filter results to the intended domain.
  6. Never pass noAiMode:false on the DataForSEO MCP for data pulls. The AI-trimmed path dropped every item from user_data; it is only safe for docs lookups.
  7. Load DataForSEO credentials the way the SEO pipeline does (Code/SEO Audit/pipeline/seo-data.mjs → Sales/_tools/keyword-data/.credentials.json). A script that reads them out of .mcp.json is blocked by the permission classifier; the pipeline path runs.
  8. A reused phone number is a citation conflict, not noise. The store's number belonged to a closed vape shop; seven pages still tie it to the old business and address. Every audit needs a phone-only query family to catch this.
  9. A [map]/local-pack item is not captured by the regular SERP endpoint. Use live/regular for cheap organic-only sweeps; switch to live/advanced only when the local pack itself is the object of study.
  10. Extended Reviews at depth 10 shows only Google. It does not enumerate third-party review platforms for a profile; do not budget it for source discovery.
  11. Do not trust site: through DataForSEO's Google SERP as a discovery layer. 102 of 128 site: probes returned zero on-domain rows, including site:yelp.com and site:weedmaps.com where the listings provably exist; Google served the loosened query instead. The unquoted form is worse. $[redacted] bought 26 usable probes. A site: miss is never absence.
  12. Do not load walled pages from an automation browser, headed or headless, and do not "try harder" against a Cloudflare interstitial. Yelp, Where's Weed, Cannapages, YP/Superpages, BBB, PotGuide, Trustpilot, TripAdvisor, Hotfrog and Manta returned 403 to plain fetch, Playwright headless-shell, and real Chrome 151 via channel:'chrome' alike (automation flag / IP reputation, not the engine). Every retry lowers the IP's standing. Prove those pages from the Google index snippet, the Wayback Machine, or the owner's own browser session.
  13. A directory search page that 404s, redirects to login, or renders under ~400 chars is a failed template, not a "not found." 22 of 38 probe not-founds were exactly that (Foursquare login wall, Nextdoor create-business redirect, Hotfrog/Brownbook/Insiderpages 404s, ShowMeLocal norobots page). Report them as unprobed with the failure, never as absence.
  14. Send a current Chrome major in the UA. MapQuest 403s Chrome/128 but serves Chrome/151 and curl-default. Pin the UA major to the launched browser's real version.
  15. Playwright 1.59.1 here wants Chromium rev 1217, which is not installed. Bare chromium.launch() throws; use launchChromium() from lib (falls back to the newest installed headless shell). Do not npx playwright install into another project's node_modules.
  16. DataForSEO OnPage instant_pages does not get past the same walls (Yelp 403 from their Hetzner gateway, $[redacted]). It proves reachability and title for open hosts only; it does not return body text.
  17. Wayback coverage is thin for directory listings. No snapshot existed for the Yelp listing. Treat Wayback as a bonus source, not a fallback you can budget on.
  18. The Google index is the cheapest verifier you already paid for. Classifying the 524 SERP titles and snippets found 17 present-match and 53 present-partial pages, including the store's real Facebook page id and four directories no registry listed, at zero additional cost. Run that pass before any fetch.
  19. A directory's search-results page echoes your query. Brand + city appearing on a search page is your own input, not a mention. Five "mention-only" rows (Yellowpages, MerchantCircle, Leafly, YouTube, Reddit) were exactly this. Only listing pages count; a search page can prove a listing exists only by linking to one.
  20. A brand regex matches strain names and other businesses. Leafly's only candidate was /strains/high-society, and Yellowpages returned "High Society" pages for Three Oaks and Union Pier. Require city plus phone or street before crediting a page; brand alone is noise in cannabis.
  21. A 403 on an unrelated page is not "blocked" for the source. A site: hit that happens to 403 (a Batteries Plus page on CitySquares, a D&B executives page) says nothing about the listing. Only a blocked search page, listing page, or NAP-query hit counts.
  22. Wayback returns PDFs as bytes. Township meeting minutes came back as %PDF-1.7 streams and classified not-found. Skip or text-extract PDFs; the Google snippet already proved the address was in them.
  23. Do not expect a Wayback snapshot for a directory listing. 45 of 81 walled URLs had none; Yelp had zero for six URLs and 403s the archiver too. Wayback proved exactly one source (Cannapages). Budget it as a bonus.
  24. Syndication shows up as identical address strings. Weedmaps, Cannamaps, The Sesh, Weedbates and the Intrinsic locator all carry "19189 W US 12", not the GBP's "19189 US-12". Fixing the address on Weedmaps fixes four downstream sites; fixing them individually is wasted work.
  25. Watch the subagent session limit on long fan-outs. Builder C was cut off by a 429 session limit the moment its pass finished; the orchestrator must be able to read every builder's outputs from disk without the builder's report.
  26. A multi-location brand pollutes its own audit. High Society has ~19 Michigan stores, so "High Society" plus a city name matches sibling locations. The Yellowpages "mention" is a Grand Rapids list page describing the Birch Run store. Never count brand-plus-city as a citation; require the phone or the street number, which are unique to the one location.
  27. Count only what the classifier can defend. The audit's own tiers are the answer: NAP-complete and NAP-partial are citations, brand mentions are not. Reporting a mention as a citation is how citation tools inflate their numbers.
  28. A guessed listing URL will promote a city page to a citation. Six sources in sources.json had hand-guessed probe.listing values that were actually locators publishing 9 to 23 different businesses' addresses. If a page carries several businesses' NAPs, it is an index entry, not a citation page.
  29. A directory's global A-to-Z dump is not proof of absence. Yellowbook returned 1,010 links and zero New Buffalo results, and an age-gate page counted as a rendered result. An absence proof must be a page scoped to the city and category.
  30. The address regex must be derived from the target's own house number. A generic street pattern needs filler characters after the number and structurally cannot match the compact 19189 US-12 form, which means it also cannot catch the wrong 19217 US-12. Build a number-specific variant or the audit misses the exact defect it exists to find.
  31. Never compare two businesses with different discovery methods. The subject got SERP queries, 82 direct probes, Wayback and a backlink pull; the competitors got SERP queries only. That produced a confident, wrong conclusion that the subject led on citations. Run the identical method on every business, or state loudly that the numbers are not comparable.
  32. Run the backlink audit on competitors, not just the subject. Most directory listings never surface in Google's index for a NAP query, but they do link. The link graph found 15 real general directories on the rank-1 competitor that the SERP sweep missed entirely.
  33. Neither discovery method is complete; union them. SERP finds indexed listings, links find listings that link back. MapQuest was found by the SERP for the subject and by the link graph for a competitor. Reporting either number alone understates every business.
  34. Split real directories from link-scheme domains before counting. One competitor showed 199 citation-like referring domains, of which 138 were machine-generated (*seodirectory.com, *rankdirectory.com, linkbuilding*). Counting them together rewards whoever bought the most links. The subject has 18 of the same junk.
  35. A zero from a URL-pattern filter is not a zero. Filtering backlinks to %new-buffalo% returned 0 for two competitors whose location URLs use a different slug. Report it as unmeasured, never as none.
  36. When a metric is suspiciously identical across every subject, suspect your reader, not the pipeline. "Exactly 5 pages" for all four businesses was Object.values() on a wrapper object with five top-level keys, not a cap and not a bug. Check the file's actual shape before reporting a defect; a false bug report costs the same trust as a missed one.
  37. A business name is not an identity; a place ID is. Three MaryJane's stores share the Local Falcon name "MaryJane's". Matching on the name put the wrong store (Etobicoke, which beats its geography) into the playbook as the worst client for four hours; the real one was North York. Join every table on place_id and print the name only for humans.
  38. A city gate that passes on incidental rows is not a city gate. Leafbuyer's /listings/dispensaries/MI/new-buffalo is a statewide dump whose title is "Dispensaries Near Me in MI"; the resolver's city_mentions >= 3 check passed on three unrelated New Buffalo rows and produced two false not-listed verdicts before an agent re-measured the page. Prove the index is city-scoped (title, breadcrumb, or a majority of rows in the city) before any absence claim from it.
  39. "Citation" and "directory listing" are the same thing measured two ways; label the method, not the concept. The statistical tests proxied citations from the backlink index (linked, indexed listings only), the hand audit fetched pages. Calling one "citations" and the other "directory listings" invented a distinction the data never made and cost a full re-cut to show 99 of 142 rows survive the relabel. Say "backlink-proxied" or "page-verified".
  40. A merge that never ran is a pipeline bug, not a finding. The control-tested priority pass listed Lume on five general directories while out-lume/verdicts.json still said undetermined for all five; the two outputs sat side by side for a day. Every later stage must consume the union, and a verifier must fail when two verdict sources disagree without a recorded winner.
  41. Vendor UTM tags in a backlink pull are a ledger; read them. utm_source=Citations&utm_medium=Dispenza&utm_campaign=T1cite on 1,549 backlinks named every store the citation vendor built for, and New Buffalo was not among them. The backlink pull had been on disk for a day before anyone grouped url_to by city. Group by destination before spending on discovery.
  42. Bing's site: operator is dead for directory probing. site:yellowpages.com "Lume Cannabis" New Buffalo returned zero results on a listing known to exist; Google's returned ten unrelated Lume stores. $[redacted] to learn; do not spend more on operator queries on either engine.
